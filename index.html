Genealogical Likelihood Under Continuous Migration

A Moment-Based Extension of gLike

Rohanna Hasselkus

University of Chicago • Summer 2026

Motivation

Demographic inference from genealogies

  • Goal:
$$ P(G|\theta) $$
  • \(G\): genealogical tree
  • \(\theta\): demographic model
  • Genealogies encode
    • population sizes
    • admixture events
    • migration history
  • ARG inference makes genealogy-based approaches computationally feasible.
  • We seek a likelihood framework for demographic inference.

gLike (Fan et al. 2025)

Genealogical likelihood framework

Given:

  • tree topology and coalescence times
  • leaf population labels
  • demographic model

Unknown:

  • population memberships of internal lineages

Compute

$$ P(G|\theta) $$

by integrating over all admissible lineage histories.

Graph of States

Central object: Graph of States (GOS)

A state specifies

population memberships of all active lineages.

Algorithm:

  • identify possible lineage populations
  • construct GOS
  • compute conditional probabilities
  • propagate marginal probabilities
  • sum over root states

Advantages:

  • exact likelihood
  • avoids explicit trajectory enumeration
  • highly accurate for pulse admixture models

Limitation

Continuous migration

Suppose migration is described by

$$ M(t) $$
  • lineages may migrate at every instant
  • configurations communicate continuously
  • the graph of states becomes intractable

The number of configurations grows as

$$ K^n $$

Question

Can we integrate over continuous lineage trajectories without enumerating states?

Structured Coalescent Likelihood

$$ L= \sum_x P(x|\mathrm{mig}) \prod_a\delta_{x_a,k_a} \prod_s \frac{1}{2N_{x_s}(t_s)} \exp\left( -\int\lambda(t;x)dt \right) $$

where

$$ \lambda(t;x) = \sum_k \frac1{2N_k(t)} \binom{\ell_k(t;x)}2 $$

Difficulty

Integrating over all trajectory assignments \(x\).

Main Idea

$$ L= L_{\mathrm{peel}} \cdot E_Q \left[ e^{-\int\lambda dt} \right] $$

This decomposition is exact.

Pass I

Compute \(L_{\mathrm{peel}}\)

Pass II

Approximate the survival term without constructing a graph of states.

Backward Pass

Felsenstein pruning under the structured coalescent

$$ L_a(k,t) = P(\text{subtree below }a\mid a\in k) $$
  • Leaf initialization
$$ L_a(k)=\delta_{k,k_a} $$
  • Branch propagation
$$ \tilde L_a(j) = \sum_k T_{jk}(\delta)L_a(k) $$
  • Coalescence merge
$$ L_c(k) = \frac1{2N_k} \tilde L_a(k) \tilde L_b(k) $$

Output

$$ L_{\rm peel} $$

Complexity:

$$ O(nK^2) $$

Why a Second Pass?

  • Backward pass gives $$ P(\text{descendants}\mid\text{location}) $$
  • Contains no information about ancestry above a node.
  • Need $$ P(\text{location}\mid\text{ancestral history}) $$
  • Hence a forward pass is required.

Forward Pass

Pairwise location priors

Track marginals

$$ \pi_{a,k}(t) $$

Track pairwise moments

$$ \Pi_{ab}(j,k;t) $$

Evolution equations:

$$ \pi_a(t+\delta) = \pi_a(t)T(\delta) $$
$$ \Pi_{ab}(t+\delta) = T^T \Pi_{ab}(t) T $$

Complexity:

$$ O(n^2K^2) $$

$$ \gamma_{ab}(j,k;t) \propto \Pi_{ab}(j,k;t) L_a(j,t) L_b(k,t) $$

Mean-Field Approximation

Only approximation in the algorithm

$$ E_Q \left[ e^{-\int\lambda dt} \right] \approx \exp \left( -\int E_Q[\lambda(t)]dt \right) $$

Therefore

$$ \log L = \log L_{\rm peel} - \int E_Q[\lambda(t)]dt $$

Everything else is exact.
